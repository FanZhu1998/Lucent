# Performance baseline, 2026-10-09

The first systematic measurement of Lucent's performance (blueprint section 10, "Performance
targets to measure"; [ADR 0021](../adr/0021-performance-baseline.md)). Every figure below comes from
the JSON in [`data/2026-10-09/`](data/2026-10-09/), rendered by `tools\bench\Format-BenchReport.ps1`;
the regression budgets derived from it are in [`budgets.json`](budgets.json). The blueprint's
figures are engineering targets, not claims, and nothing here turns them into one.

- **Build:** `release` preset of branch `feat/w12-hardening` (commit e51dd65 plus the W12 part 1
  working tree: the aggregate worker memory limit, the final check by bands, and this report's
  measurement hooks), MSVC 19.44, triplet x64-windows-lucent. Start-up runs used the installed
  layout `tools\package.ps1 -SkipBuild -StageOnly` staged (308 files, 62.9 MiB).
- **Harness:** `tools\bench\Invoke-LucentBench.ps1` (all ten scenarios, three repetitions after a
  discarded warm-up each, 56 min, 18:05 to 19:01 UTC) and `tools\bench\Invoke-BatchResilience.ps1`.
  Two supplements: `batch-cancel` was measured again at 19:03 UTC after a harness fix
  (`machine-batch-cancel.json`; the first run's third repetition found its report through a race the
  harness now handles), and the Dual Pixel CR3, which lucent-render exports only with `--subimage`,
  at 19:05 UTC (`export-dualpixel.json`, `machine-dualpixel.json`).
- **Reading the figures:** "median [min-max] (CV)" over the three recorded repetitions; with three
  values the nearest-rank p95 is the maximum. Per-image figures first take each image's median.

### A loaded development laptop, not an idle reference machine

The measurements ran on the developer's laptop with its working applications open: 366 processes,
about 18 GB committed against 11.9 GB of memory (the rest in the page file), and only 1.6 to 2.7 GB
of physical memory available before each run. During the batches the available memory fell to
0.36 to 1.6 GB, so Windows compressed and paged memory, and Lucent's admission deferred its second
worker 12 to 81 times per batch (it requires the next estimate plus 512 MiB to be free, ADR 0012).
Other processes used CPU throughout: a C/C++ language server indexing (about one processor), the
editors, a remote-desktop client and, during the two supplements, a video call. During the JPEG and
Auto batches the machine as a whole was 78 to 91 % busy while Lucent's processes used 43 to 54 %
(57 % and 25 % for the 16-bit TIFF batches). The figures are therefore conservative for throughput
and for anything that waits on memory, and their variances include this load. A baseline on an
idle machine (or the blueprint's 16 GB laptop) is the next step before these budgets are used to
judge small changes (ADR 0021).

`Lucent.exe --export-batch <folder> --preset jpeg|tiff [--automatic auto] --quit-when-done`, a new
library each run: "submission" imports and fingerprints the folder, "batch" runs from the batch's
creation to its end, photos per minute counts exported photos. The 40 fixtures export 37 (the two
Z 8 HE files and the Insta360 One X are left out as unsupported, with their reasons). Every report
was checked against the files: each exported entry's file has the receipt's size and SHA-256, no
file lacks a receipt, nothing is left in the folder.

What the batches show:
- **Two workers gain little.** The 40-fixture batch took 98.6 s (22.5 photos per minute) with up
  to two workers; the same 37 photos exported one after another by lucent-render take 102.2 s
  (sum of their medians, a process start each). Lucent's processes used about half the machine; the
  second worker was deferred for memory 12 to 20 times per 40-photo batch while available memory
  was 0.4 to 1.6 GB, and each attempt's export step is largely single-threaded (above). On this
  evidence, admission under low free memory and the export step are where batch throughput is.
- **Memory stays well inside the limit.** The workers together peaked at 1,557 MiB (JPEG) and
  1,844 MiB (with Auto) of the 4,559 MiB aggregate limit; the largest worker was the 61 MP A7R V's
  export (1,395 MiB) or an Auto check (1,403 MiB). The limit was never hit.
- **Lucent.exe grows during automatic batches:** 165 MiB of working set for a JPEG batch of five,
  887 MiB for the same batch with Auto (600 and 970 MiB for 40 photos). The analysis runs in the
  workers; what Lucent.exe holds the extra memory for was not investigated here (a follow-up).

## Variance

- **Steady (CV at most 5 %):** start-up, first RAW preview after adding a photo, Auto in the editor,
  the analysis per image (median CV 2 %), the TIFF export (4 %), the 40-photo batches (1 to 2 %),
  and most memory peaks (0 to 2 %; the start-up working set, 6 to 8 %, and the five-photo
  automatic batch's workers together, 9 %, are the exceptions).
- **Moderate (5 to 10 %):** drag medians, the 100 % view, the JPEG export per image (median CV 8 %),
  the five-photo JPEG batch (10 %). The export's variation is mostly drift, not noise: its third
  pass over the 37 images, about 16 minutes after the first, was slower for 34 of them (by a median 14 %
  for JPEG and 6 % for TIFF, against 1 % between the first two passes) as the machine's other load
  changed.
- **Noisy (above 10 %):** opening an already added photo (16 to 61 %), the one-step exposure edit
  on the Z 6 (66 and 107 ms; its third frame was published after 66 ms but never recorded as
  shown), final frames after a drag's release (9 to 30 %), Auto cancelled during the analysis
  (40 %: 34 to 80 ms), the restart thumbnail of 40 photos (19 %).
Only figures with a CV of at most 10 % (memory peaks 5 %) and three values become budgets
(decision 5 of ADR 0021, [`budgets.json`](budgets.json): 68 budgets, 11 tracked).

## The 1,000-file batch resilience run

`tools\bench\Invoke-BatchResilience.ps1`, 19:20 to 19:26 UTC (6 min), [`resilience.json`](data/2026-10-09/resilience.json).
**Every check passed.**

**The corpus.** The first attempt linked the 40 fixtures 25 times each and found that Lucent imports
a further hard link of a file it already has as a duplicate of that photo (ADR 0008: "the library's
version of a file found at another path is a duplicate while the library's path still holds it"),
so 1,000 links made 40 photos; and real copies of the fixtures for 1,000 files would need about
31 GB, against 13 GB free. The corpus is therefore 1,000 distinct files in a fixed shuffled order:
every fixture once (40 links), 100 real copies of five small fixtures (Canon 350D, R5 Dual Pixel
crop, Sony A450, DJI FC4382, Canon 5D Mark IV sRAW), 10 copies of fixtures cut to half their size,
and 850 synthetic DNGs (0.8 to 2.7 MP in five sizes, a lit scene with patches and noise, every
file its own bytes; `LucentBench.SyntheticDng`), 3.8 GiB written. Most photos are therefore small,
and the run took 6 minutes rather than hours: it shows the batch machinery at 1,000 jobs, not
1,000 large photos.

**The run.** One as-shot JPEG batch of the folder in a new library, two workers:

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Report | 1,000 entries: 987 exported, 13 skipped with their reasons (the three unsupported fixtures and the 10 truncated copies, left out at submission); the 850 synthetic files, the 100 copies and 37 of the 40 fixtures exported; every link and copy of one fixture ended the same way |
| Outputs | all 987 hashed by the watcher (each removed 60 s after it appeared, the newest left for the recovery): each has its receipt's SHA-256 and size; no file without a receipt, no output or source twice, no temporary file left |
| Injected worker crashes | at 100 and 451 photos done, a lucent-worker.exe ended with 0xC0000005 while it ran a job: both jobs were retried and exported ("the worker process ended unexpectedly", a transient failure; 2 attempts) |
| Pause and resume | paused at 250 photos done: the running photo finished 0.20 s after the request; resumed 10 s later |
| Lucent.exe ended | at 603 photos done (TerminateProcess, as a crash or a power cut would; the Job Objects ended the workers). Started again with `--resume-batch`: the recovery reclaimed the 2 interrupted attempts' leases, closed their publications and removed their 2 temporary files, requeued both, and the batch finished the remaining 382 photos (2.2 min, no further retry) |
| Cancellation | a new batch of the first 100 files in a new library, cancelled after 20 photos with one running: stopped 0.20 s after the request; report 20 exported (each file checked), 80 cancelled, no output for a cancelled photo |
| Memory, session 1 (to the kill) | Lucent.exe's commit 259 MiB in the first tenth of the samples after the first photo, 262 MiB in the last tenth (max 263 MiB); the workers together at most 1,433 MiB (sampled each second) of the 4,559 MiB limit |
| Memory, session 2 (after the restart) | 299 MiB to 302 MiB (max 302 MiB); workers together at most 934 MiB sampled, 977 MiB by the group Job Object; the limit was never hit |
| Originals | the 40 fixtures and the 110 copies have the same SHA-256 after the run |

Memory is bounded on this evidence: Lucent.exe's commit moved by 3 MiB over 600 and over 380
photos (a least-squares trend of 78 and 216 MiB per hour over runs of 3 and 2 minutes, where 3 MiB
of growth is within the measurement's own bookkeeping), and the workers' total followed the photo
being processed, not the number processed. A long run with large photos, and the disk-full and
drive-removal cases (covered by the batch tests, ADR 0012), are not part of it.

## What is not measured

- **The blueprint's 16 GB reference laptop, and an idle machine.** Everything here comes from the
  loaded 12 GB development laptop described above; budgets apply only to it.
- **A start after a reboot** (Windows' DLLs and the driver uncached); the cold start measures a
  first start after installation.
- **GPU paths:** Lucent renders on the CPU only.
- **Long interactive sessions** (hours of editing, memory over time), the editor while a batch runs,
  scrolling a library of thousands of photos, and the UI thread during batches beyond its largest
  gap per run.
- **Batches to network or removable destinations, 16-bit TIFF batches of all fixtures, Display P3,
  related-shot consistency (`--keep-consistent`) and Analyse only.**
- **Photos above 61 MP** in these scenarios (ADR 0013 measured a synthetic 100.8 MP Auto run), and
  the Insta360 One X and Nikon Z 8 HE files Lucent does not export.
- **Disk-full and drive removal** in the 1,000-file run: the batch tests cover them (ADR 0012); the
  run injects worker crashes, a pause, the end of Lucent.exe, corrupt files and a cancellation.
- **Installer and first-run costs** beyond the cold start, and memory of the Windows shell
  extensions, previews in Explorer and similar integration (Lucent has none).
